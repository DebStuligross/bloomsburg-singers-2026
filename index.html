June 2, 2026 Joe Stuligross “This Week in Bloomsburg Singers”

Greetings, Choir:

We made some solid, musical progress on Monday — isn’t it more fun to be making music now that pitches and rhythms are largely under control?!? There are still a few places, some in each piece, really, where pitches and rhythms are not 100 percent secure. I urge you to explore these based on your own individual challenges. Some specific suggestions:

  • Now I Walk in Beauty — last phrase (“and below me”) still needs the minor third to find the correct pitch on “and.” Listen carefully to the recording. We can’t take more rehearsal time to fix this.
  • Freedom Train — we will work this hard on Monday, and it will also require your independent study; pitches and rhythms were not uniform during rehearsal (we got through it only because it’s such an energetic piece, to be honest)!
  • It’s a Republic — this is weak from Letter C to the end, and especially the last 2 pages. It will be much better if you are able to bring your own confidence to rehearsal on Monday!
  • Pilgrim — awesome!
  • Wayfaring — lots of polish took place this week: crescendos on long notes, elimination of American “r” sound, purity of vowels (woe, roam, home), elimination of the fermata in m. 42. If you are not secure on the pitches on p. 5, please review.
  • Chester — be sure you’re confident of pitches if you jump around to a different part. Review the text so you can declaim it with confidence and with the youthful aspiration of building a new nation.
  • City Called Heaven — please review the expressive polishing we did this week: lengthening the pitches, building crescendos and decrescendos, rhythmic stresses.
  • Thompson, first movement — Sopranos, be brave and commit your physical being to those high notes; together as a section you will triumph! Tenors and basses, be sure you are confident of the T/B section on p. 6 (m. 29).
  • De Colores, Promise of Living, Tell My Father — we didn’t rehearse these; keep them fresh through your own study!

OK, this is a long list, and for some of you choir is not your full-time job. Please review as much as you are able — we have only one full rehearsal left, and it will be so much more fun if we’re able to be fully confident and expressive!

Monday — our final rehearsal prior to dress rehearsal — be prepared to stay a few minutes late (we need it!). Looking forward to hearing your progress (and check below for further announcements)!

Joe

Apr 19, 2026 Joe Stuligross “Bloomsburg Singers”

Dear Singers,

With 3 rehearsals down, I feel like I’m starting to get to know you all, as a group, but it will take me quite a bit longer to get to know each of you as individuals. For it is in the unique commitment of each individual that the strength of our mutual efforts will bear fruit… On this topic, I’m hoping that each of you will spend at least a few minutes each day looking at your music — 7 days is a long time to go between rehearsals, and we don’t have time to sing every piece at every rehearsal (that means 14 days!). No piano? No problem — focus on the rhythms and texts to build confidence and security. Can’t read the rhythm? No problem — just focus on the text. If each of us does what we can, we’ll all help each other to learn better and more.

Just 2 more rehearsals before A Cappella for a Cause (yikes!) — On the program for that concert are Wayfaring Stranger and (possibly) Pilgrim of Sorrow / De Colores.

On Monday, we will have sectional rehearsals again during the early part of rehearsal — I will work with the sopranos and altos, and section leaders will work with the other sections. At the end of rehearsal I’d like to work with tenors and basses to see if we can make more progress on “Tell My Father.”

See you soon!
